# Values
The "Manifesto for Agile Software Development" was created in February 2001 by representatives from various lightweight software development methodologies as an alternative to documentation-driven, heavyweight processes. It emphasizes four core values:

- **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools.
	- **Meaning**: While processes and tools have their place, the people performing the work and their direct communication and collaboration are more crucial for success.
    
- **Working software** over comprehensive documentation.
	- **Meaning**: Delivering functional software that provides value to the user is the primary goal and the best measure of progress, more so than extensive documentation.
    
- **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation.
	- **Meaning**: Continuous engagement and collaboration with the customer throughout the development lifecycle are prioritized over strict adherence to initial contractual stipulations, allowing the product to evolve with the customer's needs.
    
- **Responding to change** over following a plan.
	- **Meaning**: The ability to adapt to changing requirements and circumstances is considered more important than rigidly adhering to an initial plan, as change is seen as an opportunity for competitive advantage.
# 12 Principles
The values are supported by twelve underlying principles:

- **Customer Satisfaction**: "Our highest priority is to satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software".
- **Welcome Change**: "Welcome changing requirements, even late in development. Agile processes harness change for the customer's competitive advantage".
- **Deliver Frequently**: "Deliver working software frequently, from a couple of weeks to a couple of months, with a preference to the shorter timescale".
- **Working Together**: "Business people and developers must work together daily throughout the project".
- **Motivated Team**: "Build projects around motivated individuals. Give them the environment and support they need, and trust them to get the job done".
- **Face-to-Face Conversation**: "The most efficient and effective method of conveying information to and within a development team is face-to-face conversation".
- **Working Software as a Measure of Progress**: "Working software is the primary measure of progress".
- **Constant Pace (Sustainable Development)**: "Agile processes promote sustainable development. The sponsors, developers, and users should be able to maintain a constant pace indefinitely".
- **Good Design**: "Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design enhances agility".
- **Simplicity**: "Simplicity—the art of maximizing the amount of work not done—is essential".
- **Self-Organizing Teams**: "The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organizing teams".
- **Reflect and Adjust**: "At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior accordingly".
# Significance
The Agile Manifesto provides a foundational shift in how software development is approached:

- **Alternative to Heavyweight Processes**: It offers a clear alternative to documentation-driven, rigid, and heavyweight development processes that often struggled with high failure rates and changing environments.
- **Focus on Value and People**: It prioritizes delivering value to the customer and recognizes the importance of individuals, their interactions, motivation, and collaboration.
- **Adaptability**: It acknowledges that requirements change and that users often don't know exactly what they want until they see something working. Agile development embraces this by being responsive to change rather than strictly following a predefined plan.
- **Iterative Improvement**: By delivering working software frequently and reflecting regularly, agile approaches allow for continuous feedback and improvement.
- **Empowerment**: It promotes trust and empowers teams to self-organize and make decisions.
- **Reduces Waste**: Principles like simplicity (maximizing work not done) and focusing on working software aim to reduce the development of unused or unnecessary features.
- **Foundation for Agile Methodologies**: It serves as the philosophical basis for a wide range of specific agile methodologies such as Scrum, Extreme Programming (XP), Kanban, etc.

In essence, the Agile Manifesto guides software development towards practices that are more adaptive, human-centric, and focused on delivering valuable outcomes in complex and evolving environments.
