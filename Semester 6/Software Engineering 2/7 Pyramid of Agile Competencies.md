The Pyramid of Agile Competencies is a didactical concept that illustrates the different layers of skills and principles necessary for successful agile software development. It suggests that certain foundational practices enable higher-level collaborative behaviors, which in turn are guided by core agile values and cultural aspects. Agile development requires many different competencies on different levels.
# Technical Practices
This level forms the foundation and includes the essential engineering skills and practices that enable agility and quality in software development. Key technical practices highlighted are:

- **Testing**: Automated testing, particularly at the unit level, is considered an absolute must for ensuring good software quality.
- **Continuous Integration (CI)**: Seen as an absolute must for being able to deliver software with high frequency. CI involves regularly integrating code changes into a central repository, followed by automated builds and tests.
- **Clean Code**: Continuously paying attention to writing good, understandable, and maintainable code from the very beginning is increasingly important.
- **Other examples include**: Extreme Programming (XP) practices, Test-Driven Development (TDD), and general Automation.

These practices provide the technical stability and speed necessary for agile processes to function effectively.
# Collaboration Practices
Building upon solid technical practices, this level focuses on how team members and stakeholders work together. Effective collaboration is key to navigating the complexities of software development. The main collaboration practices are:

- **Customer and Requirements**: Intensive and frequent communication with the customer is of utmost importance. This ensures that the team understands the customer's needs and can adapt to changing requirements.
- **Other examples include**: Scrum, Iterative and Incremental approaches, and general team collaboration practices.
## Agile Champion
This refers to a catalyst leadership style, where at least one person in a project champions agility. The roles of an agile champion include:

- Leading and inspiring agility.    
- Helping define necessary changes.
- Convincing and onboarding others to support the change.
- Demonstrating positive results of the change.        
- Avoiding "cowboy" Agile and preventing regression to older methods (e.g., waterfall).
- Leading modifications to the change process.
- Removing impediments to the agile transformation.    
- Guiding the team to new levels if progress plateaus.   
## Collaboration & Communication
Intensive and open communication among all stakeholders is a key element for successful agile projects. This occurs in three major scenarios:

1. Among team members themselves.
2. Between the team as a whole and the customer/end-users.
3. Between the team and management (which might operate in a classical hierarchical way).
# Agile Values
This highest level encompasses the guiding principles, mindset, and cultural aspects that underpin and shape all agile activities. It includes:

- **Transparency and Openness**: These are highly valued in agile software development. Teams take measures to keep the organization and customers informed about progress and to obtain feedback quickly.
- **Software Craftsmanship**: This is described as more than a technique; it's an attitude, emphasizing professionalism, quality, and pride in the work produced.
- **Other examples include fundamental agile values like**: Trust, motivation, courage, and self-organization
## Organizational Culture
The context of the organization's culture impacts how agile teams operate:

1. Agile team, organization, and company (fully agile environment).
2. Agile team and organization, non-agile company.
3. Agile team, non-agile organization and company (most challenging).
