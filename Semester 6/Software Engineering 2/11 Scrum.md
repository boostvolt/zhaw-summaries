Scrum is a lightweight framework that helps people, teams, and organizations generate value through adaptive solutions for complex problems. It's an agile process allowing focus on delivering the highest business value in the shortest time.
# Values
## Commitment
The Scrum Team commits to achieving its goals and supporting each other.
## Focus
Their primary focus is on the work of the Sprint to make the best possible progress toward these goals.
## Openness
The Scrum Team and its stakeholders are open about the work and the challenges.
## Respect
Scrum Team members respect each other as capable, independent people and are respected as such by those they work with.
## Courage
Scrum Team members have the courage to do the right thing and work on tough problems.
# Roles & Responsibilities
**Composition**: Consists of one Scrum Master, one Product Owner, and Developers. There are no sub-teams or hierarchies within a Scrum Team.

**Characteristics**:
- A cohesive unit focused on one objective at a time, the Product Goal.
- **Cross-functional**: Members have all skills necessary to create value each Sprint.
- **Self-managing**: Internally decide who does what, when, and how.
- **Size**: Typically 10 or fewer people (often cited as 5-9 in supplementary material ). Smaller teams communicate better and are more productive. If larger, they should consider reorganizing into multiple cohesive Scrum Teams focused on the same product, sharing the same Product Goal, Product Backlog, and Product Owner.

**Overall Accountability**: The entire Scrum Team is accountable for creating a valuable, useful Increment every Sprint. They are responsible for all product-related activities, including stakeholder collaboration, verification, maintenance, operation, experimentation, etc..
## Developers
**Definition**: People in the Scrum Team committed to creating any aspect of a usable Increment each Sprint.

**Accountabilities**:
- Creating a plan for the Sprint (the Sprint Backlog).
- Instilling quality by adhering to a Definition of Done (DoD).
- Adapting their plan each day toward the Sprint Goal.
- Holding each other accountable as professionals.
## Product Owner (PO)
**Accountability**: Maximizing the value of the product resulting from the Scrum Team's work. Also accountable for effective Product Backlog management.

**Responsibilities for Product Backlog Management**:
- Developing and explicitly communicating the Product Goal.
- Creating and clearly communicating Product Backlog items.
- Ordering Product Backlog items.
- Ensuring the Product Backlog is transparent, visible, and understood.
- The PO may do this work or delegate it but remains accountable.

**Authority**: Is one person, not a committee. Their decisions are visible in the Product Backlog and Increment and must be respected by the entire organization for success. Represents the needs of many stakeholders. Those wanting to change the Product Backlog convince the PO. Decides on release date and content and is responsible for product profitability (ROI). Accepts or rejects work results.
## Scrum Master (SM)
**Accountability**: Establishing Scrum as defined in the Scrum Guide. Accountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness by enabling them to improve practices within the Scrum framework.

**Role**: A true leader who serves the Scrum Team and the larger organization.

**Services to the Scrum Team**:
- Coaching in self-management and cross-functionality.
- Helping focus on creating high-value Increments meeting the DoD.
- Causing the removal of impediments to progress.
- Ensuring all Scrum events are positive, productive, and within timeboxes.
- Shields the team from external interferences.

**Services to the Product Owner**:
- Helping find techniques for effective Product Goal definition and Product Backlog management.
- Helping the Scrum Team understand the need for clear and concise Product Backlog items.
- Helping establish empirical product planning for a complex environment.
- Facilitating stakeholder collaboration as requested or needed.

**Services to the Organization**:
- Leading, training, and coaching in Scrum adoption.
- Planning and advising Scrum implementations.
- Helping employees and stakeholders understand and enact an empirical approach.
- Removing barriers between stakeholders and Scrum Teams.
# Events (Ceremonies)
## Sprint
**Definition**: The heartbeat of Scrum where ideas are turned into value. A container for all other events. Fixed-length events of **one month or less** to create consistency. A new Sprint starts immediately after the previous one concludes.

**During the Sprint**:
- All work necessary to achieve the Product Goal happens (Planning, Daily Scrums, Review, Retrospective).
- No changes are made that would endanger the Sprint Goal.
- Quality does not decrease.
- The Product Backlog is refined as needed.
- Scope may be clarified and renegotiated with the PO as more is learned, without affecting the Sprint Goal.

**Purpose**: Enables predictability by ensuring inspection and adaptation of progress toward a Product Goal at least every calendar month. Shorter Sprints generate more learning cycles and limit risk. Each Sprint may be considered a short project.

**Cancellation**: A Sprint can be cancelled if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete. Only the Product Owner has this authority.
## Sprint Planning
**Purpose**: Initiates the Sprint by laying out the work to be performed. This plan is created by the collaborative work of the entire Scrum Team.

**Attendees**: Scrum Team. The PO ensures attendees are prepared to discuss important PBIs (Product Backlog Items) and their mapping to the Product Goal. Other people can be invited for advice.

**Addresses Three Topics**:
- **Topic One: Why is this Sprint valuable?** The PO proposes how the product could increase value. The whole Scrum Team collaborates to define a **Sprint Goal**. The Sprint Goal must be finalized by the end of Sprint Planning.
- **Topic Two: What can be Done this Sprint?** Developers select items from the Product Backlog to include, possibly refining them. Confidence in forecasting depends on past performance, capacity, and DoD.
- **Topic Three: How will the chosen work get done?** Developers plan the work necessary to create an Increment that meets the DoD, often by decomposing PBIs into smaller items (e.g., of one day or less). This is at the sole discretion of the Developers.

**Output**: The **Sprint Backlog** (Sprint Goal, selected PBIs, and the plan for delivering them).

**Timebox**: Maximum of **eight hours for a one-month Sprint**; usually shorter for shorter Sprints. 
## Daily Scrum
**Purpose**: To inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary, adjusting upcoming planned work.

**Format**: A **15-minute event** for the Developers of the Scrum Team. Held at the same time and place every working day to reduce complexity. If PO or SM are working on Sprint Backlog items, they participate as Developers.

**Structure**: Developers can select any structure as long as it focuses on progress toward the Sprint Goal and produces an actionable plan for the next day. (The "3 questions: What did you do yesterday? What will you do today? Is anything in your way?" is a common, but not prescribed, format ).

**Benefits**: Improves communication, identifies impediments, promotes quick decision-making, and consequently eliminates the need for other meetings. Not the only time Developers can adjust their plan.

**Participants**: Only "pigs" (committed team members) can talk; "chickens" (involved stakeholders) can observe but not interfere.
## Sprint Review
**Purpose**: To inspect the outcome of the Sprint and determine future adaptations.

**Activities**: The Scrum Team presents the results of their work (the Increment) to key stakeholders, and progress toward the Product Goal is discussed. The team and stakeholders review what was accomplished and what changed in their environment. Attendees collaborate on what to do next. The Product Backlog may also be adjusted.

**Nature**: A working session, not limited to a presentation (e.g., a demo of new features). It is informal, with a suggested 2-hour prep time rule and no slides. The world can be invited.

**Timing**: Second to last event of the Sprint.

**Timebox**: Maximum of **four hours for a one-month Sprint**; usually shorter for shorter Sprints.
## Sprint Retrospective
**Purpose**: To plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness.

**Activities**: The Scrum Team inspects how the last Sprint went regarding individuals, interactions, processes, tools, and their Definition of Done. Assumptions that led them astray are identified and explored. The team discusses what went well, problems encountered, and how they were (or weren't) solved. The most helpful changes to improve effectiveness are identified, and the most impactful improvements are addressed as soon as possible, possibly added to the next Sprint Backlog.

**Participants**: Whole Scrum Team.

**Example Technique**: Start/Stop/Continue (discuss what to start, stop, or continue doing).

**Timing**: Concludes the Sprint.

**Timebox**: Maximum of **three hours for a one-month Sprint**; usually shorter for shorter Sprints.
# Artifacts
## Product Backlog
**Definition**: An emergent, ordered list of what is needed to improve the product. It is the single source of work undertaken by the Scrum Team. Ideally expressed so each item has value to users/customers. Prioritized by the Product Owner and reprioritized at the start of each Sprint.

**Product Backlog Items (PBIs)**: Items that can be Done by the Scrum Team within one Sprint are deemed ready for selection in Sprint Planning, usually after refinement.

**Refinement**: The act of breaking down and further defining PBIs into smaller, more precise items (adding details like description, order, size). This is an ongoing activity.

**Sizing**: The Developers doing the work are responsible for sizing PBIs. The PO may influence by helping understand trade-offs.

**Commitment: Product Goal**
- Describes a future state of the product, serving as a target for the Scrum Team to plan against.
- It is in the Product Backlog; the rest of the Product Backlog emerges to define "what" will fulfill it.
- A product is a vehicle to deliver value, with a clear boundary, known stakeholders, and defined users/customers (can be a service, physical product, or abstract).
- The Product Goal is the long-term objective; the team must fulfill (or abandon) one before taking on the next.
## Sprint Backlog
**Definition**: Composed of the Sprint Goal (why), the set of Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint (what), and an actionable plan for delivering the Increment (how).

**Ownership & Visibility**: A plan by and for the Developers. It's a highly visible, real-time picture of the work Developers plan to accomplish to achieve the Sprint Goal.

**Adaptability**: Updated throughout the Sprint as more is learned. Should have enough detail for Developers to inspect progress in the Daily Scrum.

**Management**: Individuals sign up for work (never assigned); estimated work remaining is updated daily; any team member can add, delete, or change the Sprint Backlog; work emerges.

**Commitment: Sprint Goal**
- The single objective for the Sprint.
- A commitment by the Developers, it provides flexibility in the exact work needed to achieve it.
- Creates coherence and focus, encouraging teamwork.
- Created during Sprint Planning and added to the Sprint Backlog. Developers keep it in mind; if work differs from expectations, they collaborate with PO to negotiate Sprint Backlog scope within the Sprint without affecting the Sprint Goal.
## Increment
**Definition**: A concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal. Each Increment is additive to all prior Increments and thoroughly verified, ensuring all Increments work together. To provide value, the Increment must be usable.

**Creation & Presentation**: Multiple Increments may be created within a Sprint. The sum of Increments is presented at the Sprint Review. An Increment may be delivered to stakeholders before the end of the Sprint; the Sprint Review is not a gate to releasing value.

**Commitment: Definition of Done (DoD)**
- A formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets the quality measures required for the product.
- An Increment is born the moment a Product Backlog item meets the DoD.
- Creates transparency by providing a shared understanding of what work was completed.
- If a PBI does not meet the DoD, it cannot be released or presented at the Sprint Review; it returns to the Product Backlog.
- If an organizational standard for DoD exists, all Scrum Teams follow it as a minimum. Otherwise, the Scrum Team creates a DoD appropriate for the product.
- Developers are required to conform to the DoD. If multiple Scrum Teams work on a product, they must mutually define and comply with the same DoD.
# Practices
## Task Board
A visual tool used by the Developers to manage the Sprint Backlog during a Sprint. It typically shows tasks under columns like "Not Checked Out" (To Do), "Checked Out" (In Progress), and "Done!". It provides a real-time view of progress and helps identify warning signs (e.g., work not progressing, too many unplanned items)
## Burndown Chart
A graphical representation of work left to do versus time. A Sprint Burndown Chart tracks the total remaining Sprint Backlog work (often in hours or story points) day by day, visually showing progress towards the Sprint Goal.
## Definition of Ready (DoR)
It's a common practice adopted by some Scrum Teams where criteria are set for a Product Backlog Item to be considered "ready" for selection in Sprint Planning.
